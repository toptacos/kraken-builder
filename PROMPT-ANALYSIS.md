## Linguistic tells

- **“All three” / “Both”** — complete the last list. Do not ask which.
- **“All requirements”** — keel checklist (yaml, hooks, suckers, CI, Docker, catalog, SECURITY, tests).
- **“Brand it”** — ink `#0B1220`, teal `#2FD0C6`, foam, gold; tagline *One binary. Many tentacles.*; company **TopTaco**; never nickname the site “Topta”.
- **“Interactive”** — under Kraken this means prompt envelopes, not a raw TTY inside the runner.
- **“Live”** — they will accept a loopback process; they push back if you refuse a public tunnel without saying why.
- **“Etc.”** — they expect the list expanded (Slack/Teams/GChat, hour/day/rules, RE/families/devs).

## Failure modes this thread already hit

1. **Assuming crawl = integration.** Facebook, GitHub, MLS, Slack. User wants connectors; policy is inbound JSON.
2. **Short quiet videos.** Volume and length were explicit follow-ups.
3. **Name collisions.** cask → wax.
4. **Brand shorthand.** “Topta” rejected; full URL required.
5. **Scope that is remote admin.** Inlet: they asked to manage the host; safe subset shipped.
6. **Forgetting the keel** on taco v0 (no init hook, no sucker). Called out by the user.

## Prompt-engineering pattern that worked

Ship a thin arm the same day → they name the missing layer (`init`, connectors, limits, logo, profit) → fill that layer in-tree → zip. Do not restart a new architecture unless they start a new arm.
